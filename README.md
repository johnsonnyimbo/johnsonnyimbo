## 👨‍💻 About Me

Hi, I'm **Johnson Nyimbo**, a Computer Science student at the **Institute of Finance Management (IFM)** in Tanzania.

I'm passionate about working with data, understanding how information flows through systems, and building solutions that turn raw data into something meaningful.

My journey started with data analytics and business intelligence. Through practical projects, I've been developing skills in data cleaning, SQL, PostgreSQL, data exploration, and visualisation.

However, my long-term ambition is to become a **Data Engineer**.

I'm particularly interested in understanding how data is collected, transformed, stored, processed, and delivered reliably for analytics and decision-making.

I want to grow beyond analysing data to building the systems and pipelines that make reliable analysis possible.

### 🔴 1. Manchester United vs Brighton — Transfer Efficiency

**Technologies:** Python · PostgreSQL · SQL · Power BI

<a href="https://github.com/johnsonnyimbo/mufc-brighton-transfer-efficiency">
<img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/VIEW%20PROJECT-007BFF?style=for-the-badge&logo=github&logoColor=white" alt="View project"/>
</a>

An investigation into whether transfer spending translates into playing time and on-pitch performance.

The project compares Manchester United and Brighton & Hove Albion across the 2022/23–2025/26 seasons.

**Questions explored:**
- How much did each club spend and earn through transfers?
- Do expensive signings become regular first-team players?
- Is there a relationship between transfer fees and player contributions?
- How much of each club's playing time goes to new signings?

**What this project demonstrates:**
- Data extraction and preparation using Python.
- Relational database design and SQL analysis.
- Data modelling and analytical thinking.
- Communicating findings through Power BI dashboards.

**Development:** Phase 1 focuses on transfer and playing-time analysis. Phase 2 will introduce deeper player-performance statistics.

## 🔄 My Approach to Data Projects

I believe good data work requires more than writing queries or creating charts.

My preferred workflow is:

1. **Understand** — Define the problem and the questions to answer.
2. **Collect** — Identify and obtain relevant datasets.
3. **Clean** — Handle missing values, duplicates, and inconsistencies.
4. **Transform** — Structure data into useful formats.
5. **Analyse** — Use SQL, Python, and analytical methods to investigate patterns.
6. **Visualise** — Present results through dashboards and reports.
7. **Communicate** — Explain the findings and their significance.

As I progress towards Data Engineering, I want to become increasingly involved in the processes that make this entire workflow reliable and scalable.
